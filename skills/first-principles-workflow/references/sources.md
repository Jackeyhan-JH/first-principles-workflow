## The five steps

Starbase tour with Everyday Astronaut, Part 1, uploaded 2021-08-03. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t705r8ICkRw

> [13:36] "First make your requirements less dumb, your requirements are definitely dumb. It does not matter who gave them to you. It's particularly dangerous, if a smart person gave you the requirements, because you might not question them enough."

> [16:00] "Whatever requirement or constraint you have, it must come with a name, not a department. 'Cause you can't ask the departments, you have to ask a person, and that person who's putting forward the requirement or constraint must agree that they must take responsibility for that requirement."

> [13:54] "...then try very hard to delete the part or process. [...] If you're not occasionally adding things back in, you are not [deleting] enough. The bias tends to be very strongly towards, let's add this part [or] process step in case we need it. But you can basically make in case arguments for so many things"

> [17:18] "And then only the third step is simplify or optimize."

> [17:26] "...possibly the most common error of a smart engineer is to optimize the thing that should not exist."

> [21:30] "...step four, which is accelerate cycle time. You're moving too slowly, go faster, but don't go faster until you've worked on the other three things first."

> [21:57] "And then the final step is automate."

> [22:02] "...I have personally made the mistake of going backwards on all five steps multiple times. [...] on Model 3. Where literally I automated, accelerated, simplified and then deleted."

The case was fiberglass mats between the Model 3 battery pack and the floor. He sped up the robot that placed and glued them before asking what they were for. The battery team said noise and vibration; the noise and vibration team said fire safety. A microphone in a car with the mats and one in a car without could not tell them apart [22:21-24:33].

> [24:37] "So we just deleted them and just bypass[ed] this $2 million robot cell"

> [24:49] "Another mistake that has to happen in production is too much in-process testing."

Lex Fridman Podcast #438, 2024-08-02. https://lexfridman.com/elon-musk-and-neuralink-team-transcript/

> [00:44:08] "I have this very basic first principles algorithm that I run kind of as a mantra, which is to first question the requirements, make the requirements less dumb. [...] You have to start there, because, otherwise, you could get the perfect answer to the wrong question."

> [00:46:20] "Look, we're deliberately going to delete more than we should. At least one in 10 things, we're going to add back in."

## First principles

Elon Musk with Kevin Rose, uploaded 2012-09-08. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L-s_3b5fRd8

> [22:38] "I think it's also important to reason from first principles rather than by analogy. So the normal way that we conduct our lives is we reason by analogy. We're doing this because it's like something else that was done, or it's like what other people are doing. [...] first principles is kind of a physics way of looking at the world, and what that really means is you kind of boil things down to the most fundamental truths and say, okay, what are we sure is true, or as sure as possible is true, and then reason up from there."

His example [23:37-25:26]: people said battery packs would always cost about $600 per kilowatt hour because they always had. Asking what a battery is made of (cobalt, nickel, aluminum, carbon, polymers, a sealed can) and what those cost on the London Metal Exchange gave about $80 per kilowatt hour.

TED2013, interview with Chris Anderson. https://www.ted.com/talks/elon_musk_the_mind_behind_tesla_spacex_solarcity/transcript

> [19:21] "boil things down to their fundamental truths and reason up from there, as opposed to reasoning by analogy. Through most of our life, we get through life by reasoning by analogy, which essentially means copying what other people do with slight variations."

Aristotle, Posterior Analytics I.2, 72a5-8, translated by G. R. G. Mure (MIT Internet Classics Archive). https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/posterior.1.i.html

> "A 'basic truth' in a demonstration is an immediate proposition. An immediate proposition is one which has no other proposition prior to it."
